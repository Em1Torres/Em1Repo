**Weak password**

The following are weak passwords:
- 123456
- admin
- password
- Name123

Usually are short, common words that also use personal information and in simple patterns.

**Intermedium password**

Its main characteristics are: 

- more difficult but still vulnerable
- Example: John@2000
- It uses uppercase/lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols, but it is still based on a predictible factor like name or year or both.

**Strong password**

The most important thing about them is that it is a long, unique, and difficult to guess. For instance: Riverv!Cloud7-Mango$Train

Here are some tips for us to create strong passwords:

- Make it long, aim for 14+ characters.
- Use a passphrase, combine unrelated words.
- Mix characters, use upper and lower case, numbers and symbols
- Avoid personal information
- Avoid patterns
- Use a unique strong password for each account.

RULE: long + unique + random

**Guessing vs. Cracking**

Theres two ways of hacking passwords:

- Password guessing: you make an analysis on someones personal data that could be found in social media, and based on some information related to dates, family members. Hackers can guess the password.

- Password cracking: The attcker uses a systemic or automated process to find the password. One example is a Tornado attack that basically tries use a patters and spams it until it finds the answer (001, 002, ... , 999).

**Crack Password methods**

- Brute force: tries many combinations until one of those works. Short passwords are very vulnerable to this type of attack.
- Dictionary attack: tries a large list of common passwords and words. So basically try to crack it via common passwords.
- Credential stuffing: so basically works on leaks that can be obtained from small websites where hackers can get your password and if its the same for multiple accounts of other sites they can rob you from all your accounts.


Website information like passwords and username are stored inside databases and we may think we can access the database and determine the password but usually this are stored by hashing tables so when looking inside databases you're looking at the hash_value from the password. Kinda works like: you enter your password in the front-end when sending to database it becomes or its given a key to access it called hash; and from it you can't go backwards into the password, its called one-way.

- Password hash cracking: people get the hash values and try comparing the password tries to the hash_ values they're looking for.
